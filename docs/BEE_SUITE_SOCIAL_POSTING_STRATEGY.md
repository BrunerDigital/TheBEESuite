# The BEE Suite Social Posting Strategy

Updated: August 20, 2026

This strategy turns the current BEE Suite social creative pack into a repeatable organic publishing system. It is a planning and content artifact only; connecting accounts, scheduling posts, spending money, or publishing still requires separate approval.

## The job of social media

Social should make The BEE Suite recognizable as the childcare operating system built around real school roles and real school days. The first 30 days should optimize for qualified attention and message clarity—not raw follower count.

Primary audience:

- Multi-location childcare owners and executives
- Center directors and operations leaders
- Childcare billing and implementation teams
- Education leaders who influence software decisions

Primary action: visit `https://thebeesuite.io` and explore the product.

## Positioning and voice

Use a confident, practical, operator-to-operator voice. Lead with a familiar childcare operations problem, show the relevant workflow, and close with one low-pressure action. Avoid generic technology language, exaggerated transformation claims, unsupported savings figures, and cute bee language when the subject is billing, safety, access, or compliance.

The campaign pillars are:

1. Connected school day — one operating workspace across roles.
2. Classroom to family — clear daily-report and parent workflows.
3. Role-safe by design — the right view for each role.
4. Multi-location clarity — executive visibility without losing school context.
5. Connected billing — tuition and payment context attached to the right records.
6. Deliberate rollout — school and feature readiness treated as separate gates.
7. Connected versus fragmented — a side-by-side operating-model comparison.
8. Cost of fragmentation — a transparent, user-modeled planning scenario.
9. Feature highlights — six connected workflows shown as one operating system.
10. Mr. BEE — human-reviewed AI assistance for summaries, drafts, and prioritization.
11. Customer stories — a testimonial-ready format used only after quote and attribution approval.
12. Inquiry to enrollment — continuity across the complete family journey.
13. Director command center — the daily signals and follow-up that need attention.
14. Reporting with context — school detail connected to executive and FTE review.
15. Parent portal — a mobile-first view of approved family information.
16. School readiness — a saveable implementation checklist with evidence gates.
17. Mr. BEE director briefing — a concrete human-reviewed AI use case for priorities and drafts.

## Expansion content rotation

Use the five expansion concepts to add variety without turning the feed into a generic feature catalog:

- `connected-vs-fragmented`: open with a recognizable handoff problem, then show the connected model.
- `model-fragmentation-cost`: send readers to `https://thebeesuite.io/brand/the-bee-suite/marketing/current/savings-calculator.html` and describe it as a planning model, never a guaranteed savings claim.
- `six-connected-workflows`: spotlight one card in the caption while the visual communicates the broader system.
- `meet-mr-bee`: focus on summarizing, drafting, explaining, and prioritizing; always retain the human-review requirement.
- `share-your-bee-suite-story`: invite operators to email `support@thebeesuite.io` with the subject “My BEE Suite story” until an approved quote, speaker name, role, organization, and usage permission are documented. Replace it with an attributed testimonial only after that review.

Rotate one expansion concept into each week after the initial 30-day foundation. Comparison and feature content can run in feed placements; the savings model works best as a link post; Mr. BEE is suited to a short screen-led demo; the customer-story card works best as a direct community prompt.

The second expansion wave adds six more reusable post roles:

- `inquiry-to-enrollment`: use as an educational carousel or journey post for enrollment teams.
- `director-daily-command-center`: pair with a “what do you check first?” operator question.
- `reporting-with-context`: use for leadership content about preserving school-level meaning in rollups.
- `parent-portal-one-place`: use as family-experience product proof without implying access is active for every school.
- `school-readiness-checklist`: optimize for saves and shares; each checklist item can become a follow-up post.
- `mr-bee-director-briefing`: demonstrate one reviewable prompt-and-draft flow at a time and retain the human-review message.

## Channel roles

### LinkedIn

Make LinkedIn the primary B2B channel. Publish two feed posts per week: one operational insight and one product proof post. Use the landscape asset when linking to the site and the portrait asset when the graphic itself should carry the post. Keep captions between 80 and 180 words, use three or four relevant hashtags, and invite a substantive response with a question no more than once per week.

### Instagram

Publish two feed posts and two or three Stories per week. Prefer the 1080 x 1350 portrait assets for feed visibility. Stories can reuse the 1080 x 1920 exports with a single native poll or question sticker placed outside the headline and CTA safe zones. Use captions that are shorter and more conversational than LinkedIn, but preserve the operational point.

### Facebook

Publish two posts per week, normally reusing the Instagram feed creative with a slightly more explanatory caption. Favor operator communities, school leadership relevance, and link clicks. Do not duplicate the same caption word-for-word across Facebook and Instagram on the same day.

### Reels and TikTok

Start with one short video per week after the static campaign establishes the brand. Use a 12–20 second screen-led format: problem hook, two or three product frames, one takeaway, then the matching story export as the end card. Do not imply that a workflow is active for every school or that the product makes legal, medical, safety, custody, billing, or compliance decisions.

## Caption framework

Use this three-part structure for new posts:

1. Problem: name one operational friction in plain language.
2. Proof: describe or show one BEE Suite workflow that addresses it.
3. Action: invite the reader to explore the product or share how their team handles the problem.

Example:

> A school day should not have to be reconstructed across disconnected systems. The BEE Suite keeps director operations, classroom workflows, family updates, billing readiness, and reporting in one role-safe workspace. See how the pieces connect at TheBeeSuite.io.

## Community and response workflow

- Check comments and direct messages once each business day.
- Answer product questions in plain language and link to the most relevant public page.
- Move account-specific, billing, family, child, medical, custody, access, or school-record questions into an approved private support workflow; never troubleshoot them in public comments.
- Hide or report spam. Escalate legal, safety, media, or reputational issues before replying.
- Keep a lightweight log of repeated questions; turn recurring questions into future content.

## Measurement

Review performance every two weeks and make creative decisions after four weeks unless a clear policy or brand issue requires immediate action.

Track:

- Qualified site visits by channel and campaign ID
- LinkedIn saves, meaningful comments, and click-through rate
- Instagram shares, saves, profile visits, and Story completion rate
- Facebook link clicks and meaningful comments
- Reel/TikTok three-second holds, average watch time, and completion rate
- Demo, contact, or qualified inquiry conversions when tracking is approved

Use consistent UTM fields at publishing time:

- `utm_source`: `linkedin`, `instagram`, `facebook`, or `tiktok`
- `utm_medium`: `organic_social`
- `utm_campaign`: the campaign ID from the asset manifest
- `utm_content`: `square`, `portrait`, `story`, `landscape`, or the video hook ID

## Optimization rules

- If saves and shares are strong but clicks are weak, keep the topic and strengthen the CTA.
- If clicks are strong but qualified inquiries are weak, tighten audience targeting and landing-page alignment before changing the creative system.
- If one pillar underperforms twice, test a new opening hook before replacing the visual.
- Turn the strongest static post into a short screen-led video the following week.
- Do not boost a post or activate paid media until the destination, tracking, audience, budget, and approval owner are confirmed.

## Production checklist

Before each post:

- Confirm the selected file matches the platform placement.
- Verify logo, headline, CTA, and product screenshot remain intact.
- Use only privacy-safe demo imagery.
- Proofread the final platform caption and UTM link.
- Confirm the post does not imply unsupported results, universal availability, autonomous decision-making, or an activated school workflow.
- Obtain the required publishing approval and preserve the final caption, asset filename, destination, and scheduled time in the content log.
